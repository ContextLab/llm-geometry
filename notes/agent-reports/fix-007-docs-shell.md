## F1 — Back/Forward (medium) · FIXED

**Judgement: the behaviour was wrong, not the docstring.** The store's own opening
paragraph lists three defects it exists to fix — no shareable link, reload loses the tab,
"**and Back does nothing**" — and then shipped with the third one intact. Correcting the
docstring would have meant deleting a stated goal to match an implementation, which is the
wrong direction. So `replaceState` became `pushState`.

The problem the old comment described is *not* reintroduced. It said tab switching "should
not fill the back stack with every click"; the push is guarded by
`if (window.location.hash !== target)`, so it is **one entry per navigation, not per
click** — re-selecting the tab already showing pushes nothing. That is pinned by its own
test, so the guard cannot be dropped as a "simplification".

`URL → store` now listens for `popstate` as well as `hashchange` (a history traversal
fires the former; a fragment typed into the address bar fires the latter), and an
`applyingFromUrl` flag stops the subscriber from pushing a duplicate entry for a
navigation the browser already performed.

- **Where:** `code/frontend/src/lib/stores.ts`
- **Tests:** `tests/unit/shell.test.ts` →
  `hash router > moves between tabs with Back and Forward`,
  `hash router > does not push an entry for re-selecting the tab already showing`
- **Before:** `Error: timed out waiting for Back to Geometry (hash=#lexicon)` — Back left
  the tab chain entirely, exactly as the red team observed.
- **After:** both pass; Back walks `#lexicon → #geometry → #architecture` and Forward
  walks back up.
- **e2e (not executed):** `docs.spec.ts > Info tab > Back and Forward move between tabs,
  as the shell documents`.

## F11 — the Dolch sublists (low) · RESOLVED, by reading the article

The red team's disposition (*unknown, not wrong*) was correct, and it is now **known**. I
obtained the full 1936 article — E. W. Dolch, *A Basic Sight Vocabulary*, The Elementary
School Journal 36(6):456–460 — as a scan of the JSTOR PDF (stable URL
`http://www.jstor.org/stable/995914`) and read all five pages. Findings, from the pages
themselves:

1. **The 95-noun list IS in the 1936 article.** Page 459: *"That the reader may see this
   fact for himself, the list of nouns is given on page 460."* Page 460 prints it under the
   heading *"95 NOUNS COMMON TO THE THREE WORD LISTS BUT NOT RECOMMENDED FOR A BASIC SIGHT
   VOCABULARY"*, and `Santa Claus` is its last entry (bottom of the third column). The
   "Why 314 and not 315" section is therefore resting on a verified source, not on
   Wikipedia.
2. **The graded sublists are NOT in the 1936 article.** The 220 words are printed on
   pp. 458–459 under *"A BASIC SIGHT VOCABULARY OF 220 WORDS, COMPRISING ALL WORDS, EXCEPT
   NOUNS, COMMON TO THE WORD LIST OF INTERNATIONAL KINDERGARTEN UNION, THE GATES LIST, AND
   THE WHEELER-HOWELL LIST"* and grouped by **part of speech** — conjunctions, prepositions,
   pronouns, adverbs, adjectives, verbs. There is no grade-level partition anywhere in the
   article, and no pre-primer / primer / first-grade heading.

The Info tab prose was reworded to say only what is supported: the 220 service words and
the 95 nouns come from the 1936 article; the split that gives the **40 / 92 / 133** budgets
does not, it is the conventional grading that has travelled with the list since, and **we
have not established where it was first published**. (I did not assert *Problems in
Reading*, 1948 — Wikipedia mentions the book but does not say the grading originated
there, and I have not seen it.) The citation is kept and strengthened rather than dropped:
the reference entry now states which pages supply what, and that the article does not
contain the grade split.

- **Where:** `code/frontend/src/viz/info/InfoTab.svelte` (the `Dolch` bullet under "Two
  kinds of budget, at the same size"; the "Why 314 and not 315" paragraph; the reference
  entry under "Source & references")
- **Verification:** the quotations above are transcribed from the article pages. No test —
  but `docs.spec.ts` still asserts `"40 / 92 / 133 / 220 / 314"` and `"314"` appear, and
  both do.
